A well-organized knowledge base is one where customers find the right article without needing to type a single word into the search bar. Thoughtful structure — built around how your customers think, not how your internal teams are organized — reduces support tickets, speeds up onboarding, and builds trust in your product. This guide walks you through planning your category hierarchy, creating and nesting categories, using tags for cross-cutting topics, and keeping your content tidy over time.

## Planning Your Category Structure

Before you create a single category, step back and map out how your customers move through your product. The most common mistake is mirroring your internal org chart — for example, creating categories named after teams like "Engineering" or "Customer Success." Your customers do not know or care how you are structured internally; they only know the problem they are trying to solve.

Instead, organize categories around **customer journeys and goals**:

- **Getting Started** — articles for brand-new users completing their first setup.
- **Account & Billing** — everything related to plans, invoices, and profile settings.
- **Using [Core Feature]** — one category per major product area, named after the feature as the customer knows it.
- **Troubleshooting** — a dedicated space for error messages, known issues, and fixes.
- **Integrations** — guides for connecting your product with third-party tools.
- **Security & Privacy** — policies, data handling, and compliance topics.

Aim for five to eight top-level categories. Too few and articles become hard to find inside a massive list; too many and the navigation feels overwhelming. A shallow, wide structure (more top-level categories with fewer articles each) is almost always easier to navigate than a deep, narrow one.

## Creating and Nesting Categories

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Category Manager">
    In your Knowledge Base dashboard, click **Content** in the left sidebar, then select **Categories**. The Category Manager displays your existing category tree.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a top-level category">
    Click **New Category**. Enter a short, customer-facing name (for example, "Getting Started") and an optional description that appears in your portal's category listing. Click **Save**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a subcategory">
    Hover over the parent category you just created and click the **+ Add Subcategory** icon that appears to its right. Give the subcategory a name that narrows the topic (for example, "Inviting Your Team" under "Getting Started"). Click **Save**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign articles to the category">
    Open any article in the editor and use the **Category** dropdown in the Article Settings panel to assign it to your new category or subcategory. You can also bulk-assign articles from the Articles list view by selecting multiple articles and choosing **Set Category** from the bulk actions menu.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Reorder categories in the navigation">
    Back in the Category Manager, drag and drop categories into your preferred order. The order you set here is exactly what customers see in the portal's left-hand navigation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Using Tags for Cross-Cutting Topics

Tags let you connect articles that share a theme but live in different categories. Unlike categories — which place an article in exactly one location — a single article can carry as many tags as are relevant.

Good use cases for tags include:

- **Product area** — tag articles with the name of a feature so customers can filter by it on the search results page (for example, `reporting`, `api`, `mobile`).
- **Audience** — distinguish content intended for admins versus end users with tags like `admin-only` or `end-user`.
- **Status** — use a `beta` or `legacy` tag to surface context without renaming the article.
- **Integration** — tag articles with the name of a third-party tool (for example, `slack`, `salesforce`) so customers following an integration-specific journey can find all relevant articles at once.

To add a tag to an article, open the article in the editor and type into the **Tags** field in the Article Settings panel. Press **Enter** after each tag. Tags are created on the fly — no pre-configuration required.

## Sorting and Ordering Articles Within a Category

By default, articles within a category are sorted by the date they were last updated. You can override this with a custom order:

1. Open **Content → Categories** and click on the category you want to manage.
2. Switch the sort setting from **Automatic** to **Manual**.
3. Drag articles into your preferred reading order. Put foundational or high-traffic articles at the top so customers encounter them first.
4. Click **Save Order**.

Use manual ordering for structured sequences like getting-started guides, where the reading order matters. Leave high-volume categories like Troubleshooting on automatic sort so recently updated articles (which are most likely to address current issues) always surface at the top.

<Tip>
  Connect your Knowledge Base analytics to your search data at least once a quarter. Open **Analytics → Search Terms** and look for searches that return zero results or have high drop-off rates. These are gaps in your content or navigation. When you see customers searching for a phrase that does not match any of your category names, consider renaming or adding a category to match the language they actually use.
</Tip>
