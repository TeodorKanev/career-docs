Every great knowledge base starts with a well-written article. Whether you are documenting a new feature, answering a common support question, or building an onboarding guide, the article editor gives you everything you need to go from a blank page to a polished, published help page — without any coding or design skills required.

## Creating an Article

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Articles section and click New Article">
    In your Knowledge Base dashboard, navigate to **Articles** in the left sidebar. Click the **New Article** button in the top-right corner. A blank article editor opens immediately — no template selection required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your title and body using the rich text editor">
    Click into the **Title** field and type a clear, descriptive headline. Then click into the body area and start writing. The rich text editor behaves like a modern word processor — just type and format as you go. Use plain language and focus on one topic per article.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add images, videos, and code blocks">
    Enrich your article with visuals and examples. Click the **+** icon in the editor toolbar (or press `/` to open the block menu) and choose:
    - **Image** — upload a screenshot or drag a file directly into the editor.
    - **Video** — paste a YouTube, Vimeo, or Loom URL to embed a video player.
    - **Code block** — insert a formatted, syntax-highlighted snippet. Select the language from the dropdown that appears on the block.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set category, tags, and SEO metadata">
    Open the **Article Settings** panel on the right side of the editor. Assign the article to a **Category**, add relevant **Tags**, and fill in the **SEO title** and **Meta description** fields. Good metadata improves both in-portal search and external search engine visibility.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Preview your article">
    Click **Preview** in the top toolbar to see exactly how your article will appear to customers in your Help Center portal. Check formatting, image sizing, and link behavior before you publish. Use the browser back button to return to the editor and make any final adjustments.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish or save as draft">
    When your article is ready, click **Publish** to make it live immediately. If you need more time or a second review, click the dropdown arrow next to **Publish** and select **Save as Draft**. Draft articles are invisible to customers but fully accessible to your team.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Editor Toolbar Overview

The toolbar at the top of the editor gives you quick access to all formatting options. Here is what each control does:

| Control | What it does |
|---|---|
| **H1 / H2 / H3** | Insert a heading to break your article into scannable sections |
| **Bold / Italic** | Emphasize key terms or cautions inline |
| **Bulleted list / Numbered list** | Present items or sequential steps in a structured format |
| **Link** | Highlight text and insert an internal or external hyperlink |
| **Image / Video** | Embed visual media directly in the article body |
| **Code block** | Insert a multi-line, syntax-highlighted code sample |
| **Divider** | Add a horizontal rule to visually separate major sections |
| **Table** | Insert a data table with resizable columns |

You can also press `/` anywhere in the body to open the full block menu and search for any element by name.

## Tips for Writing Clear, Scannable Articles

Customers rarely read help articles word for word — they scan for the answer they need. These habits make your articles easier to follow:

- **Keep paragraphs short.** Aim for two to four sentences per paragraph. A wall of text is harder to scan than a series of focused points.
- **Use numbered steps for any process.** If a task has a sequence, number each step so customers can follow along without losing their place.
- **Lead with the action.** Start step descriptions and bullet points with a verb ("Click", "Enter", "Select") so the instruction is immediately clear.
- **Add a screenshot for every major UI step.** A single annotated image often communicates faster than a paragraph of description.
- **Use headings generously.** Every major topic shift deserves its own H2 or H3 heading. This also improves SEO by surfacing relevant keywords.
- **Write for your least technical reader.** Avoid jargon, spell out acronyms on first use, and link to related articles rather than assuming prior knowledge.
- **End with next steps.** A short "What's next?" section or a set of related article links keeps customers moving toward their goal.

<Note>
  Your Knowledge Base automatically saves a new version every time you publish an update to an article. To view or restore a previous version, open the article in the editor, click the **⋯ More options** menu in the top-right corner, and select **Version History**. Each version is timestamped and shows the name of the team member who made the change.
</Note>
