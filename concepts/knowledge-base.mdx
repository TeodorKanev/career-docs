A knowledge base is a centralized, searchable library of information that helps your customers and teammates find answers on their own — without waiting for a support agent or digging through email threads. Think of it as your product's single source of truth: a living collection of articles, guides, and reference material organized so that the right answer is always a few clicks away.

## Knowledge Base vs. Wiki vs. FAQ

Not all self-service content is the same. Understanding the differences helps you choose the right format — and avoid mixing them up in ways that confuse readers.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Knowledge Base" icon="book-open">
    Customer-facing, structured, and search-optimized. Designed to help users solve specific problems or learn how to use your product. Content is curated, versioned, and maintained by a dedicated team.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Wiki" icon="file-lines">
    Typically internal and collaborative. Anyone on the team can create or edit pages. Great for capturing institutional knowledge, meeting notes, and processes — but less suited for polished customer-facing content.
  </Card>
  <Card title="FAQ Page" icon="circle-question">
    A flat list of common questions and answers. Easy to create but hard to scale. FAQs become unwieldy as your product grows, and they lack the navigational structure customers need when they have complex problems.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Anatomy of a Knowledge Base

Every well-built knowledge base shares a common set of building blocks. Understanding each component helps you plan your content strategy before you write a single word.

<Accordion title="Articles">
  Articles are the individual help documents that answer a specific question or explain a specific topic. Each article has a title, a body, a category assignment, tags, and optional SEO metadata. Articles can be how-to guides, troubleshooting walkthroughs, conceptual explanations, or reference material.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Categories">
  Categories group related articles together so customers can browse by topic rather than relying solely on search. A well-designed category structure mirrors the mental model your customers already have — not the way your internal teams are organized.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Search">
  Full-text search is often the first thing a customer reaches for. Your knowledge base automatically indexes article titles, body content, tags, and metadata so customers can find answers in seconds. Search analytics also show you which queries return no results — a direct signal of content gaps.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Help Portal">
  The help portal is the customer-facing website where your knowledge base lives. It includes a homepage, a search bar, category navigation, and individual article pages. You can customize the portal's branding, layout, and domain to match your product.
</Accordion>

## Best Practices for Structuring a Knowledge Base

A well-structured knowledge base saves your support team time and genuinely helps customers. Follow these principles to set a strong foundation:

- **Organize for your customers, not your company.** Name categories and articles using the words your customers actually type into search — not your internal product names or team jargon.
- **Start with your top support tickets.** Before writing anything new, audit your recent support conversations. The questions that come up most often are the articles you need first.
- **Keep each article focused on one task or concept.** Avoid mega-articles that try to cover everything. Short, focused articles are easier to find, easier to read, and easier to keep up to date.
- **Build a review cadence from day one.** Stale content erodes trust faster than no content at all. Assign article owners and schedule regular reviews so your knowledge base stays accurate as your product evolves.

<Note>
  Content accuracy is just as important as content coverage. Set a recurring reminder — monthly or quarterly — to audit your most-visited articles and flag anything that no longer reflects your current product. You can use the **Last Reviewed** field on each article to track when content was last verified.
</Note>
