Categories are the folders of your knowledge base — they group related articles into logical sections so customers can browse by topic when they're not sure exactly what to search for. A well-designed category structure acts as a map of your product: customers scan the homepage, recognize a familiar topic area, click in, and find the article they need. Get categories right and your help portal feels intuitive from the very first visit.

## What Categories Are and How They Appear in the Portal

A category is a named container that holds one or more articles. On your help portal's homepage, categories appear as tiles or list items — each showing the category name, an optional icon, and a short description. When a customer clicks a category, they see a filtered list of every published article assigned to it, organized by the sort order you define.

Categories also appear as breadcrumbs on individual article pages (`Home > Category > Article Title`), giving customers context for where they are and an easy path back to browse related content. If you use subcategories, those appear as nested sections within the parent category page.

## Parent Categories and Subcategories

Your knowledge base supports two levels of nesting: **parent categories** and **subcategories**.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Parent Categories" icon="folder">
    Top-level containers that appear on your portal homepage. These should represent broad topic areas — think "Getting Started," "Billing," "Integrations," or "Account Settings." Aim for five to ten parent categories to keep the homepage from feeling overwhelming.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Subcategories" icon="folder-open">
    Second-level containers nested inside a parent category. Use subcategories when a parent category has enough articles to warrant further grouping. For example, an "Integrations" parent category might have subcategories for "CRM," "Email," and "Payments."
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Info>
  Nesting is capped at two levels (parent → subcategory). This constraint is intentional — deeper nesting creates navigation that customers find confusing and hard to backtrack through. If you feel the urge to create a third level, that's usually a sign the parent category needs to be split into two separate top-level categories instead.
</Info>

## Choosing Category Names

The names you give your categories have a direct impact on how quickly customers find what they need. Follow these guidelines when naming categories:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use your customers' language">
    Name categories using the words your customers actually say, not your internal product terminology. If customers call it "billing" but your team calls it "revenue operations," name the category "Billing."
  </Step>
  <Step title="Be specific, not clever">
    Avoid abstract or branded names that only make sense to people who already know your product well. "Getting Started" is universally understood. "Your Journey Begins" is not.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use nouns, not verbs">
    Category names work best as topic labels (nouns) rather than action phrases (verbs). "Account Settings" is better than "Manage Your Account." Save action-oriented language for article titles.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check your search analytics">
    The queries customers type into your search bar are a gold mine for category naming. If dozens of customers search "invoice," make sure the word "invoice" appears in your Billing category's name or description.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Category Settings

Each category has a set of configurable fields that control how it looks and behaves in your portal.

<Accordion title="Icon">
  Choose an icon from the built-in icon library to visually represent the category on the portal homepage. Icons help customers scan and orient themselves faster, especially on mobile. Pick icons that have a clear, universal meaning — a credit card for Billing, a wrench for Troubleshooting, a rocket for Getting Started.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Description">
  A short sentence (one to two lines) that appears below the category name on the homepage tile. Use the description to clarify what kinds of articles live in the category. Example: "Find answers about invoices, payment methods, plan upgrades, and refunds."
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Visibility">
  Set a category to **Public**, **Logged-in customers only**, or **Internal**. Internal categories and all articles within them are never shown on the help portal. This is useful for staging new content sections before they're ready to launch.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Sort Order">
  Control the order in which articles appear within the category. Choose **Manual** to drag and drop articles into your preferred sequence, or **Alphabetical** / **Most Recent** for automatic ordering. Manual sort order gives you the most control over the reading experience, especially in Getting Started categories where sequence matters.
</Accordion>

<Warning>
  Resist the temptation to create a category for every sub-topic. A knowledge base with thirty thin categories — each holding only one or two articles — is harder to navigate than one with eight well-populated categories. If a category has fewer than three published articles, consider merging it into a related parent category or promoting those articles to a broader section. Quality of organization beats quantity of categories every time.
</Warning>
