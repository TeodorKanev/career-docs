Articles are the core unit of your knowledge base — each one answers a specific question, explains a concept, or walks a customer through a task. Every article you publish adds a page to your help portal, a document to your search index, and a resource your support team can link to directly. Getting your articles right is the highest-leverage investment you can make in your self-service experience.

## Article Types

Not every piece of content serves the same purpose. Choosing the right article type from the start helps you write more focused content and helps customers know what to expect before they start reading.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="How-To Guide" icon="list-check">
    Step-by-step instructions that walk a customer through completing a specific task. Use numbered steps, screenshots, and clear action verbs. Example: "How to invite a team member."
  </Card>
  <Card title="Troubleshooting" icon="wrench">
    Helps customers diagnose and fix a problem. Typically structured as a series of symptoms and solutions. Example: "Why isn't my payment processing?"
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reference" icon="table">
    Factual, scannable information customers look up rather than read end-to-end. Includes things like setting definitions, API field descriptions, and keyboard shortcuts.
  </Card>
  <Card title="FAQ Entry" icon="circle-question">
    A concise answer to a single frequently asked question. Best used sparingly and organized within a dedicated FAQ category rather than scattered across your knowledge base.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Article Lifecycle

Every article moves through a defined lifecycle from first draft to eventual retirement. Understanding each stage helps your team collaborate effectively and ensures customers only ever see polished, approved content.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft">
    The article exists but is not visible to customers. Writers use this stage to create and iterate on content without publishing anything unfinished. You can save a draft as many times as needed.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review">
    The article is ready for a second set of eyes. Assign a reviewer — a subject-matter expert, an editor, or a support lead — to verify accuracy and clarity before the article goes live. Leave inline comments directly on the article body.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Published">
    The article is live on your help portal and indexed by search. Customers can find and read it. You can still make edits to a published article; changes save immediately and update the live page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Archived">
    The article is no longer visible to customers but is preserved in your account for reference. Use this stage for content that is outdated or superseded by a newer article rather than deleting it outright.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Key Article Fields

When you create or edit an article, you'll fill in several fields that control how the article appears in your portal and how easy it is to find.

<Accordion title="Title">
  The title appears as the article heading on the portal page and as the link text in search results and category listings. Write titles that describe the outcome or answer — not just the topic. "Reset your password" is better than "Password management."
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Body">
  The main content of your article, written in the rich text editor. Supports headings, paragraphs, lists, images, code blocks, callout boxes, tables, and embedded videos.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Category">
  Every article must belong to at least one category. The category determines where the article appears in your portal's navigation. You can assign an article to multiple categories if it genuinely belongs in more than one place, but avoid doing this by default.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Tags">
  Free-form labels that improve search discoverability. Tags are not visible to customers but are indexed by the search engine. Use tags to capture synonyms, common misspellings, and related product terms.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="SEO Metadata">
  The **SEO title** and **meta description** fields control how your article appears in external search engines like Google. If left blank, the portal uses the article title and an excerpt from the body. Customizing these fields can meaningfully increase organic traffic to your help content.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Visibility">
  Control who can read the article. Options include **Public** (anyone with the portal URL), **Logged-in customers** (requires authentication), and **Internal** (only visible to your team in the editor). Internal articles are never published to the portal.
</Accordion>

<Tip>
  Write article titles the way your customers would search for them. If a customer is frustrated and typing quickly, they'll search "can't log in" not "authentication troubleshooting." Use your search analytics to see the exact phrases customers type and match your titles to those patterns.
</Tip>

## Rich Text Formatting Options

The article editor gives you a full set of formatting tools so you can present information in the clearest possible way. Use formatting intentionally — heavy formatting can fragment content and make it harder to read.

- **Headings (H2, H3)** — Break long articles into scannable sections. Use H2 for major sections and H3 for subsections.
- **Ordered and unordered lists** — Use numbered lists for sequential steps and bullet lists for non-ordered items or options.
- **Bold and italic** — Bold for UI labels, key terms, and warnings. Italic for titles, new terms being defined, or light emphasis.
- **Images and screenshots** — Annotated screenshots dramatically reduce the cognitive load of procedural content. Upload images directly or paste them from your clipboard.
- **Code blocks** — Inline code for short snippets and commands; fenced code blocks with syntax highlighting for longer samples.
- **Tables** — Ideal for comparing options, listing fields with descriptions, or showing structured reference data.
- **Callout boxes** — Note, Tip, Warning, and Info callouts draw attention to important information without interrupting the flow of the main content.
- **Embedded videos** — Paste a YouTube or Loom URL to embed a video player directly in the article body.
